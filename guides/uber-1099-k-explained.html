Uber 1099-K explained: why it shows more than you were paid

Your 1099-K says one number. Your bank deposits say a smaller one. Here's why, and how not to overpay.

Short answer: the 1099-K reports the gross amount riders paid, before Uber's service and booking fees. Report that gross amount as income, then deduct the fees as a business expense. Skip the fee deduction and you pay tax on money you never received.

Which forms you might get

1099-K: sent when payments are over $20,000 and 200 transactions (some states lower). IRS 1099-K FAQs

1099-NEC: Uber sends this for promotions, referrals and other non-trip pay of $2,000 or more. Uber tax FAQ

No form? The income is still taxable. All gig income counts, whether or not a form arrives.

What the Uber tax summary helps with

The yearly tax summary isn't an official tax form, but it lists your online miles and the fees that may be deductible, like service fees, booking fees, tolls and airport fees. Use it alongside your own mileage log.
